Why this practice exists

Built from the receiving end.

This did not start as a software idea. It started with commissioned study work that came back as a folder of documents and spreadsheets, with analytics that could not be retraced to the data underneath — and then having to defend those results to investors and boards anyway.

The standard the pipeline was built to meet comes directly from that experience: one comprehensive report a client can actually use, every number traceable to how it was computed, delivered in hours rather than days. We build it the way we wanted it delivered to us.

One study, from bench to report

Most analytics vendors have only ever sat at the computer.

Tractum has stood at the bench that generates the data, at the computer that turns it into evidence, and at the client's desk that receives the report. The pipeline is built to serve all three.

Act 1 — The bench

A study wraps, and the data is rich but inert

High-content images, numerical assay readouts, the protocol that defines what any of it means, and the metadata tying every file to subject, group and timepoint. Heterogeneous, scattered, and not yet evidence.

Act 2 — The old path

Days of manual wrangling, and a report that hides its working

Images graded by hand, assay numbers pasted between tools, ad-hoc statistics, thin methods. What the sponsor receives is a folder — PDFs, spreadsheets, loose figures — with analytics that cannot be retraced to the data underneath. Meanwhile the scientists sit at a keyboard instead of the bench.

Act 3 — The pipeline

Read the protocol first, then analyse in context

The pipeline ingests protocol and metadata before anything else, so it knows the design, the arms, the endpoints and the acceptance criteria. Agentic data flows then carry the grind: each modality processed correctly, the right statistics applied for the design every time, and the report generated from the analysis rather than assembled by hand. The scientist keeps the quality gate — automation removes the labour, never the judgement.

Act 4 — The deliverable

Not a folder. One report.

Accurate results, clear analytics, publication-grade figures, and fully transparent methods where every value maps back to how it was computed — days of preparation reduced to hours. The sponsor, who knows the assay cold, can audit it, trust it, and take it to their board.

Act 5 — The compounding edge

A better method drops in once, and every future study inherits it

The pipeline is modular, so improvements are made centrally rather than per project, and new services — transcriptomics, advanced imaging — plug into the same frame. Because the analytics are built by people who understand the biology, they are shaped to the disease question rather than to a generic off-the-shelf readout.
