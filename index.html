Why the eye

71.6% vs 52.0%of Phase I programs, where safety and tolerability are tested, advanced to Phase Iwe: ophthalmology vs all indications, 2011 to 2020. The highest rate of any disease area (BIO/Informa/QLS).
55%of new drugsof novel active substances launched in the past decade were originated by emerging biopharma, not large pharma. The ones that reached patients were usually commercialised by a bigger company (IQVIA Institute).
US$66Bby 2030projected global ophthalmic drug market, up from an estimated US$37.5B in 2025, with investment concentrated in sight-threatening disease such as AMD and diabetic retinopathy (Mordor Intelligence; Cheng et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2025).
2.9x vs 1.7xreturn on investment from pre-clinic to approval for companies whose lead drug is developed across more than one indication, versus a single indication: 21% a year against 11%. At Phase 1 those companies were valued at US$594M against US$230M. From 311 biopharma acquisitions, 2005 to 2020 (Michaeli et al.).
US$1.3BupfrontMerck’s 2024 EyeBio deal, plus up to US$1.7B in milestones. Buying in is how large pharma most often builds an eye pipeline.

Managing Director & Founder

Dr. Adrian Cioanca

Retinal scientist, biotech co-founder and strategy consultant. I help companies frame the development question early, and build the evidence in the order that answers it.

I spent my research years on retinal degeneration: why photoreceptors die, and what might stop them. From inside the lab I saw good science stall for reasons outside the experiment. The development question was framed late, the evidence was built in the wrong order, or the results never reached a board in terms it could act on.
