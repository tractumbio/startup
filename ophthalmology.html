Why ophthalmology

The eye is a particularly attractive environment for drug development and asset repositioning.

7.9%  all-indication baseline

Development odds that can favour the eye

In BIO's analysis of 14 disease areas, ophthalmology ranked among the highest for cumulative likelihood of approval from Phase 1, against a 7.9% all-indication average. The edge sits in early-phase progression and regulatory approval; Phase III success (51.2%) is near the cross-industry average (57.8%).

BIO / Informa / QLS Advisors — Clinical Development Success Rates, 2011–2020 (comparison group: all indications)
$20–40B  2025, scope-dependent

A large market with value concentrated in retina

Estimates place the ophthalmic drugs market in the tens of billions in 2025, growing roughly 6% a year — with the greatest value in retinal disease, where therapies command strong pricing.

MarketsandMarkets; Mordor Intelligence, 2025 (ranges reflect differing drug / device scope)

Local delivery, a wider therapeutic window

Delivering directly to the eye can create high drug exposure at the target tissue while limiting systemic exposure — potentially widening the therapeutic window for an asset that struggled systemically.

Ocular pharmacology principle

You can actually see the response

The eye is accessible for direct imaging, functional testing, biomarker measurement, and repeated monitoring — allowing a clearer, earlier read on biological and clinical response than most tissues permit.

Established clinical practice — OCT, ERG, visual function endpoints

Systemic assets often carry untapped ophthalmic potential

When the mechanism is relevant to retinal, inflammatory, vascular, fibrotic, neurodegenerative, or anterior-segment disease, an asset developed elsewhere may already have a credible home in the eye. Established regulatory pathways, validated endpoints, and specialist clinical networks make well-validated ophthalmic assets attractive to partners and acquirers.

Pharmaceutical demand is real and recent

$1.3B
upfront · up to $3B including milestones

In 2024, Merck acquired the ophthalmology company EyeBio for $1.3 billion upfront and up to $3 billion including milestones — for a clinical-stage candidate targeting diabetic macular edema and wet AMD. It is one of several recent moves by large pharmaceutical companies to acquire and license validated ophthalmic assets rather than build the biology in-house.
