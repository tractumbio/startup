The framework

Built on the agentic AI mesh

We do not invent an architecture for each client. We use the agentic AI mesh, the pattern McKinsey’s QuantumBlack published for running agents at enterprise scale, and we bring the laboratory knowledge that tells each agent what good looks like in a study.

Why a tested pattern matters

Most laboratory automation fails the same way: a clever script, written by one person, that nobody else can run, audit or extend. The mesh exists because large organisations hit that wall at scale. It sets out how agents are registered, what each is permitted to touch, how they pass work between themselves, and how every action stays on the record.

Why the biology matters more

The architecture is the easy half. An agent that segments a retina, judges a dim ERG trace or picks the model for a repeated-measures design needs someone who has run those studies to define correct. That is the part we bring, and it is the part a generalist automation vendor cannot.

The agentic AI mesh is an architectural pattern published by McKinsey’s QuantumBlack in Seizing the agentic AI advantage, built on five principles: composability, distributed intelligence, layered decoupling, vendor neutrality and governed autonomy. We are not affiliated with McKinsey or QuantumBlack. We follow the published pattern and prefer open standards, so nothing we build ties you to one vendor.
