How the engagement runs

Three tracks, with a decision after each. Stopping early is a valid outcome, and usually the cheapest one. We use software to search the literature faster, but the recommendation is ours.

Track 1 · Assess

Is the eye a credible target?

  • Matching your mechanism to eye diseases it could treat
  • Literature review and computational assessment
  • Whether the drug can reach the target tissue in the eye
  • A first read of the patent landscape

You get: a shortlist of indications, an evidence matrix, a risk register and a recommendation memo.

Track 2 · Design evidence

Prove it

  • A plan for the safety, toxicology and efficacy studies
  • Endpoints and acceptance criteria
  • Writing the CRO brief, choosing the provider, overseeing the work

Who does what: we design the studies and oversee them. They run at CROs you contract directly.

Track 3 · Position

Defend it

  • IP strategy with your patent attorney
  • A valuation model with every assumption written down
  • Materials for investors and partners

Who does what: formal freedom-to-operate opinions and filings come from a qualified patent attorney, not from us.

References

The figures we quote, and where they come from. Industry forecasts are third-party projections; the peer-reviewed analyses below are the primary evidence.

  1. Wong CH, Siah KW, Lo AW. Estimation of clinical trial success rates and related parameters. Biostatistics 2019;20(2):273–286. doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxx069. From 406,038 trial records: ophthalmology moved from Phase 1 to Phase 2 in 87.1% of trials against 66.4% across all areas, and the overall probability of success from Phase 1 to approval was 32.6% against 13.8%.
  2. BIO, Informa Pharma Intelligence and QLS Advisors. Clinical Development Success Rates and Contributing Factors 2011–2020 (February 2021), Figure 2. Phase I transition success by disease area: 71.6% in ophthalmology against 52.0% across all indications.
  3. Michaeli DT, Yagmur HB, Achmadeev T, Michaeli T. Valuation and Returns of Drug Development Companies: Lessons for Bioentrepreneurs and Investors. Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science 2022;56(2):313–322. doi:10.1007/s43441-021-00364-y. Across 311 acquisitions from 2005 to 2020, equity in companies with multi-indication lead drugs returned a 2.9x investment multiple from pre-clinic to approval against 1.7x for single-indication drugs, or 21% a year against 11%.
  4. Michaeli DT, Yagmur HB, Achmadeev T, Michaeli T. Value drivers of development stage biopharma companies. European Journal of Health Economics 2022;23(8):1287–1296. doi:10.1007/s10198-021-01427-5. Acquirers paid a 12% premium per additional indication, and 37% more for biologic and gene therapy lead drugs than for small molecules.
  5. IQVIA Institute. Emerging Biopharma’s Contribution to Innovation (2022). Share of novel active substances originated outside large pharma, 2012 to 2021.
  6. Merck & Co. “Merck to Acquire EyeBio”, 29 May 2024. US$1.3B paid upfront, with the lead candidate in early clinical development.
  7. Cheng H, et al. Ophthalmic drug discovery and development using artificial intelligence and digital health technologies. npj Digital Medicine 2025;8, 25 September 2025. Ophthalmic market projection and early-stage development time.

Reference 1 reports clinical-phase transitions across all sponsors from 2000 to 2015; reference 2 covers 2011 to 2020 on a different dataset and method. Both are historical industry comparisons and neither forecasts the outcome of any individual candidate. Reference 3 models returns from acquisition values combined with published success rates, and its ophthalmology data sit within a combined “other” disease category.
