How the engagement runs

A deliberate plan, from first question to a package a partner can act on.

Three phases, run in order. Phase 1 ends in a decision, and that decision is genuinely two-way — most assets should not proceed, and finding that out early is the cheapest result available. Nothing in Phase 2 begins until you have chosen to continue.

Phase 01 · Relevance

Is the eye real for this asset?

Does the biology hold — and if it does, in which indication?

  • Deep literature research across the target, pathway and ocular tissue evidence
  • In-silico analytics to surface and rank candidate indications at speed
  • Mechanism deep dive: delivery route, tissue relevance, translational evidence
  • Indication screening on unmet need, feasibility and commercial viability
  • Competitive and freedom-to-operate landscape read

You receive A ranked shortlist of ophthalmic indications, the evidence behind each, what would disprove it, and a recommendation.

Phase 02 · Evidence

Prove it, with the right studies

What evidence would settle this, and who is qualified to generate it?

  • Safety, toxicology and efficacy study plan built around decision thresholds
  • Model selection, endpoints and effect sizes that would count as success
  • CRO identification, scoping and selection for the work required
  • Oversight through execution — protocols, quality gates, troubleshooting
  • Interpretation of the results and how to position them

You receive A completed evidence package, interpreted, with the findings framed for the audience that matters next.

Phase 03 · Position

Defend it, and make the case

Can it be protected, and can a partner act on it?

  • IP strategy: freedom to operate, and new intellectual property the work creates
  • Support through drafting and the filing process, alongside your attorneys
  • Valuation modelling — transparent, tunable, every input visible
  • Investor and partnering materials aimed at large pharma
  • Preparation for the diligence questions those audiences will ask

You receive A defensible IP position and a package your board, investors or a pharma partner can act on.

Gate · after Phase 01

Go or no-go, before serious capital is committed. A no-go is a complete result, not a failed engagement — you end Phase 1 with a clear scientific and commercial rationale for not pursuing the eye, and the capital you would have spent finding that out the expensive way.

Automation and analytics accelerate the search and keep the evidence current. They do not make the call. The judgement at this gate is a scientist's, and it is the reason the rest of the plan is worth running.

Pharmaceutical demand is real and recent

$1.3Bupfront · up to $3B total

In 2024, Merck acquired the ophthalmology company EyeBio for $1.3 billion upfront and up to $3 billion including milestones — for a clinical-stage candidate targeting diabetic macular edema and wet AMD. It is one of several recent moves by large pharmaceutical companies to acquire and license validated ophthalmic assets rather than build the biology in-house.
